# Extraction — Gutachten: Auswirkungen der Vergesellschaftung privater Wohnungsunternehmen in Berlin

Source: `pdf/Gutachten-Auswirkungen-der-Vergesellschaftung-privater-Wohnungsunternehmen-in-Berlin.pdf` → markdown (31 pages). Cover page references Deutsche Kreditbank (DKB) — reads as a bank/creditor-risk-perspective impact study, more recent than the other documents (cites a 2026 "Rahmengesetz" and a 2025 Landesrechnungshof report), critical of below-market compensation from a financial-stability angle.

## Data

- **Updated compensation estimates cited (post-2023, superseding earlier figures elsewhere in the corpus):**
  - Official Senate "Amtliche Kostenschätzung" (2021, market-value-based): **€29–39 billion**
  - DW&E "Faire-Mieten-Modell" (updated/revised, per Holm et al. 2025): **≈€8–11 billion** (down from the €14.5bn figure DW&E itself cited in 2021/22 — extract note: reconcile which is more current for the model)
  - Reference market-value transaction: Vonovia → HOWOGE (Land-owned) sale of **4,500 Wohnungen for €700 million in 2024**, cited as matching book value (Buchwert/Verkehrswert) almost exactly — offered as empirical proof Verkehrswert ≈ actual transaction value (i.e., not inflated/speculative).
  - Threshold framing: "geplante Entschädigungssummen von unter 15 Milliarden Euro" treated as the realistic low-end planning range for compensation.
- **Ownership structure data:** typical Fremdkapitalquote (debt ratio) of the targeted companies ≈ **70%**. Named international owners: Heimstaden (~20,000 Berlin units; Scandinavian investors incl. pension funds Alecta, Folksam, Swedish state pension agency), Covivio (~17,000 Berlin units; French REIT, owned partly by Italian family offices and insurers/banks Crédit Agricole, BNP Paribas, Generali, Société Générale), plus Adler Group, Pears Global, Grand City Properties (majority international ownership).
- **Berlin fiscal data:** Landesschulden 2024 = **€67 billion**; projected end-2027 = **€76 billion** (Landesrechnungshof Berlin 2025). A **+0.5 percentage-point** risk premium on Berlin's financing costs is estimated to cost **≈€400 million/year** in added interest expense.
- **Historical precedent — France 1981/82 nationalizations (Mitterrand):** 10-year OAT–Bund sovereign spread rose from ~3.3pp (1979) to 5.4pp (1980, pre-election) to 6.2pp (1981, after Mitterrand's win) to a peak of **7.1 percentage points** at the height of the bank/industry nationalizations; spread receded only after nationalizations were reversed and austerity imposed (Sachs/Wyplosz 1984). French Conseil Constitutionnel (Jan 1982) struck down the government's bank-compensation formula for ignoring inflation and true asset value ("wahrer Substanzwert"), forcing a revised, higher compensation calculation.
- **Historical precedent — Chile 1970s:** US expropriations triggered a US-led capital-market boycott of Chile.
- Comparator: GDP/tax-revenue modeling elsewhere in the doc (¶1015, not fully extracted here) references a scenario of BIP +€2bn / public revenue +€240m — likely a *counterfactual growth* estimate from an alternative policy, worth revisiting if a full GDP-side module is built.

## Relational / Modeling

- **Core causal chain modeled (qualitative but readily formalized):**
  `Entschädigung < Marktwert → Kreditausfallrisiko bei Bestandsbanken (weil Grundpfandrechte erlöschen und durch schuldrechtlichen Anspruch ersetzt werden) → Risikoprämien auf Neukredite ↑ → Investitionstätigkeit (Bestand + Neubau) ↓`
  and, in parallel:
  `Vergesellschaftung mit Entschädigung < Marktwert → wahrgenommenes Länderrisiko Berlin ↑ → Spread auf Landesanleihen ↑ → Zinskosten Land Berlin ↑ → Haushaltsspielraum ↓ → (via Länderfinanzausgleich) Ansteckung auf andere Länder/Bund`
- **Bank-exposure formula implied (¶1252–1258):** given typical leverage ≈70% debt, `Bankverlust ≈ max(0, Kreditvolumen − Entschädigungssumme)` per affected company, i.e. a compensation figure below ~70% of Verkehrswert directly implies uncovered bank losses (and potential Pfandbrief-default risk) rather than just an equity-side loss to shareholders — a distinct risk channel from the compensation-vs-shareholder-fairness framing in the other documents.
- **Sovereign-risk sensitivity relation (calibratable from the French data point):** a +0.5pp risk-premium scenario ⇒ +€400m/year interest cost on Berlin's existing ~€67–76bn debt stock — i.e. roughly `ΔZinskosten/Jahr ≈ Schuldenstand × Δrisk_premium` (linear approximation; the France case shows the premium itself is highly state-contingent and can reach several percentage points at the peak of a nationalization episode, i.e. this linear read is a lower-bound scenario, not a worst case).
- **Compensation-vs-book-value benchmark:** the 2024 Vonovia→HOWOGE transaction (€700m / 4,500 units ≈ **€155,556/unit**) is offered as a real, recent arm's-length Verkehrswert data point for Berlin multifamily portfolios — directly usable as a per-unit market-value sanity check against any Ertragswert/DCF output the model produces, and independently cross-checkable against the Liegenschaftszinssatz/Vervielfältiger data in `05-02-010-2500.extract.md`.
- Net effect claimed: below-market compensation does not just redistribute from owners to the AöR/state, but propagates into **higher system-wide financing costs** (mortgage lending, sovereign borrowing) that could offset the intended affordability gains — a feedback loop worth representing explicitly if the model's scope extends beyond a single-portfolio valuation into system-level financing effects.
